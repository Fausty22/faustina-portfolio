Where Customer Success Meets Technology: Why Customer Success and Technology Belong in the Same Conversation

For a long time, I thought of my career as a collection of different experiences.

Customer support. Administrative work. Customer Success. Operations. Technology. Cloud and DevOps.

On the surface, they may look like separate paths. But the more experience I gained, the more I realised that there is a connection between all of them: people, problems, and technology.

I've always enjoyed figuring things out.

When a customer has a problem, I want to understand what is actually causing it, not just provide a quick response.

When a workflow isn't working, I want to understand where the process is breaking down.

And when I'm working with technology, I don't just want to know which button to click. I want to understand what is happening behind the system and why.

From supporting people to understanding systems

My professional background started from a people-focused place. With a degree in Education and experience across customer-facing and administrative roles, I developed skills in communication, organisation, problem-solving, and working with different kinds of people.

Customer Success added another layer to that.

I began to understand that supporting a customer isn't simply about answering questions or resolving complaints. It's about understanding what the customer is trying to achieve and helping them get there.

A customer can have every support ticket resolved and still not be successful.

They might not be using the product properly. They might not understand an important feature. Their needs might have changed. Or they might simply not be getting the value they expected when they signed up.

That changed the way I think about customer support. I became more interested in what happens before and after the support ticket.

What caused the problem? Is it happening repeatedly? Is the customer actually adopting the product? Could we identify the problem earlier? And what does the customer ultimately need to achieve?

Then I started looking deeper into the technology

My interest in Cloud and DevOps came from a similar place: curiosity about what happens behind the scenes.

I started working with technologies such as AWS, Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform, GitHub Actions and Argo CD.

One of my major projects was deploying a three-tier banking application on AWS using infrastructure as code, containerisation, Kubernetes and CI/CD.

Working on that project gave me a different perspective. I wasn't just looking at technology as a user anymore. I was beginning to understand the systems that power the products people depend on.

And that made my Customer Success experience even more meaningful.

When you're supporting a technical product, understanding the technology behind it can change the quality of the conversation. You don't necessarily need to be the person writing every piece of code. But you should be able to understand the problem, communicate clearly with technical teams, translate technical information for customers, and keep the customer's actual goal in view.

The intersection is where I want to grow

Today, I'm interested in the space where Customer Success and technology meet.

That includes Customer Success, Client Onboarding, Technical Account Management, Technical Support, cloud technologies and the operational processes that connect them.

I enjoy being the person who can look at a customer problem from both sides:

What is the customer experiencing?

and

What is happening behind the technology or process that is causing it?

I think that combination is increasingly important as more businesses rely on software and cloud-based products.

Customers don't just need someone who can communicate well. They need people who can understand the product, ask the right questions, work across teams, solve problems, and keep the customer's desired outcome at the centre of the conversation.

That's the kind of customer-facing technology professional I am building my career around.

Why I created this blog

This website brings the different parts of my professional work together.

I'll be sharing perspectives and practical work across Customer Success, Technical Account Management, technology, cloud and DevOps, customer experience, and the lessons that come from working across customers, products and technical teams.

The goal is to make the work behind these areas clear, practical and useful.

But because I believe there is value in documenting the process of building a career at the intersection of different disciplines.

Some posts will be about Customer Success. Some will be technical. Some will be about projects I've worked on and the problems I encountered. And some will simply be reflections on working with customers, technology and teams.

If you're interested in any of those things, I hope you'll find something useful here.

For me, this is more than just a blog. It's a place to document the work, problems and ideas that shape how I approach customers, products and technology.

Welcome to my corner of the internet.
